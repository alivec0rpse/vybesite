Tell us the reader before you tell us the card

The single most expensive mistake in a card order is buying the wrong frequency. A 125 kHz reader will never read a 13.56 MHz card no matter how good the card is, and a panel configured for MIFARE Classic will not read DESFire unless somebody reconfigures it.

So send us a photograph of the reader on your door or turnstile, or its model number, before anything is ordered. It takes a minute and it is the difference between cards that work on the morning they arrive and a box of very well-printed coasters.

In detail

A card is only as good as what is on the chip.

Which chip, and why it matters more than the price

An EM4100 card at 125 kHz is cheap, read-only and carries a number anybody with a fifteen-hundred-rupee cloner can copy in seconds. That is genuinely fine for a store room. It is not fine for a payroll gate, a hostel door or a cafeteria wallet. MIFARE DESFire EV2 and EV3 are a different class of thing: mutual authentication, encrypted communication, and no meaningful cloning risk in practice. The honest advice usually lands between the two: put the good chip where the money or the safety is, and do not pay for it on the visitor pass.

The default keys problem

Most card systems that get broken into are not broken by cryptography. They are broken because the MIFARE sector keys were left on the factory default, which is published in every datasheet and known to everybody. It costs nothing to set your own keys at encoding time and it is the single highest-value thing you can do to a card programme, so we do it by default and tell you what was set. If you have an existing estate still on factory keys, that is worth fixing before you buy a single new card.

Personalisation is a data job, not a printing job

The printing is the easy part. What determines whether a run of two thousand student cards goes smoothly is the quality of the spreadsheet and the photographs: consistent columns, names that match the enrolment system, photographs named by the same identifier as the rows. Where that is clean, cards are printed, encoded, verified and boxed in order. Where it is not, somebody spends three days matching faces to names, and that somebody is going to be one of us. We will tell you exactly what format to send before you start collecting it.

One card, several systems

The point of choosing the chip properly is that one card can do everything. The same card opens the gate, marks attendance in Vybe ERP, pays for lunch through Vybe CMS, borrows a book and gets the student into the exam hall. That only works if the format, the numbering and the keys are agreed once at the start, across every reader and every system that will touch the card. Retrofitting a second system onto a card programme that was not planned for it is where most campuses end up carrying two cards and resenting both.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

How is this different from the cards under Printing?

add

It is the same physical card, approached from opposite ends. Card printing is about the artwork, the finish and short runs: visiting cards, plain ID cards, a hundred passes for an event. This page is about what happens once a chip is involved: which chip, at which frequency, encoded to which system, with which keys, matched to the readers you already own. If your card has to open a door, mark attendance or pay for lunch, you are on the right page. If it just has to look good, the printing page is the cheaper one.

Will these work with the readers we already have?

add

That is the first thing to settle and it is why we ask for the reader model before quoting. A 125 kHz reader will never read a 13.56 MHz card, and a MIFARE Classic reader will not read DESFire unless it was set up for it. Tell us what is on your doors and turnstiles, and a photograph of the unit is usually enough, and we will tell you which chip you need. Where a campus is mid-migration between two systems, a dual-frequency card buys you the time to finish.

Do you encode them, or do we?

add

Whichever you prefer, and most people prefer us to. Cards encoded before delivery arrive working and matched to your data, with the printed number and the chip number agreeing, and we hand back a file your system can import. If your security policy says the keys never leave your building, that is a good policy and we will supply the cards blank with the numbering you specified.

Can they work with Vybe ERP and the cafeteria system?

add

Yes, and that is a large part of why we make them. Cards for a school running Vybe ERP come encoded for attendance, and cards for a campus on Vybe CMS come ready for the cashless cafeteria terminals. One card usually does the gate, the classroom and the canteen. Because both ends are ours, there is nobody in the middle explaining why the format does not match.

What is the minimum quantity?

add

Just 25 cards. A small office, a clinic or a single hostel block should not have to buy four years of cards to get this year's, so a run of 25 is a normal order with us, and so is the top-up of 25 for the next batch of joiners.

How long do they take?

add

Plain encoded cards from stock are quick. Fully personalised cards, with photo, name, number, encoded and verified, depend on how fast the data and the photographs reach us, and that is almost always the slow part rather than the printing. Send a clean spreadsheet and named photograph files and you will have a realistic date; send four hundred photographs called IMG_0423.jpg and it will take longer, for reasons nobody can fix at our end.

What does it cost?

add

RFID cards start at ₹16.50 a card and NFC cards at ₹50 a card, plus GST, from a minimum of 25. The RFID price is for a card printed with your design, not a blank, and they are Bengaluru's lowest prices for premium cards. The chip moves the price more than anything else, so a DESFire EV3 card costs more than an entry-level one. Tell us the chip or the reader, the quantity and what has to go on the face of the card at sales@vybe.net.in and the exact figure comes back.
