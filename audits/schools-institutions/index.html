Where an audit names a standard — FSSAI Schedule 4, the National Building Code, POCSO, the POSH Act, the DPDP Act, the UN SDGs — that names the yardstick we read you against. In detail

School, college & institute audits

Most campuses buy these together, and should

The audits above are separated because they answer to different rules, not because they are different visits. The walk that finds a fire exit chained shut is the walk that finds the chemistry store unlocked, the camera pointing at a wall and the mess storing rice on the floor. Doing them in one go costs less than doing them in four, and it produces one report with one fix list rather than four that contradict each other on priority. Where a campus wants only one, it is usually fire, and that is a reasonable place to start.

Fire is where the gap is widest

Almost every campus we have walked has extinguishers. Rather fewer have extinguishers of the right type in the right places with current refill dates, and fewer still have anybody on the floor who has held one. The pattern repeats: the hardware exists because somebody bought it, and the practice does not because nobody owns it. Drill records are the clearest tell. A campus that drills twice a year, times it, and writes down what went wrong is a campus that will be fine in an inspection and, far more to the point, in a fire. A campus with a drill photograph from 2022 is neither.

The child safety audit is the uncomfortable one

It has to be. Police verification is where it usually starts and it is usually incomplete — teaching staff are verified, the housekeeping contractor’s people are not, and the van driver is somebody’s cousin. Then there is the policy, which exists as a PDF nobody has read, and mandatory reporting under section 19 of POCSO, which a surprising number of staff do not know binds them personally. We ask these questions of the people who would actually receive a disclosure from a child, because asking the principal tells you what the school intends rather than what it does. Where we find something serious, you will hear it from us immediately and in plain words, not in a report three days later.

Why the SDG audit is not a generic green audit

Because a generic green audit produces a folder about waste bins, and an accreditation team asks a different question. NAAC wants sustainability evidenced across governance, practice and, critically, curriculum — what students are actually taught and what they actually do — with a policy, a committee, a budget line and closed actions behind it. NIRF wants numbers you can defend. So the audit is built to produce that: energy, waste and water measured rather than described, every finding mapped to the specific Sustainable Development Goal and target it answers to, and the evidence assembled in one file instead of reconstructed the week before a submission. Institutions that already have a green audit report usually find the gap is not the environment work, it is that none of it is written down in a form anybody can score.

What you get, and what it is worth

A written report per audit with photographs, delivered within a few working days of the visit. Every finding cites the rule, the code clause, the guideline or the Goal it is read against, so it can be argued with on its merits rather than taken on our say-so. Findings are graded, the fix list is ordered, and where the audit closes at or above our threshold you get a Vybe certificate and a digital badge. What that certificate is not is an NOC, an accreditation, an affiliation document or anything carrying an authority’s mark. FAQ

Questions, answered.

Does the fire safety audit get us a fire NOC?

add

No, and be careful of anybody who says theirs does. A No Objection Certificate is issued by the state fire services department after its own inspection — in Karnataka, the Karnataka State Fire and Emergency Services. We are not the fire department, we are not affiliated to it, and we cannot issue or arrange a clearance. The Vybe Fire Safety Certificate records that we audited you to the NBC standard and what we found; it is not an NOC and must not be presented as one. What the audit does is find the things that would hold a clearance up, while there is still time and budget to fix them.

Is the SDG audit recognised by NAAC or NIRF?

add

No. We have no affiliation with NAAC, NIRF, the UGC or any accrediting body, and nothing we issue carries marks or standing with them. "NAAC/NIRF-ready" describes how the audit is built — measured, evidenced and mapped to specific Sustainable Development Goals, in the structure those frameworks ask questions in — so that your team is not assembling it from scratch at submission time. The assessment, the score and the accreditation remain entirely theirs to give.

Which buildings actually need a fire clearance?

add

Part 4 of the National Building Code 2016 sets the thresholds by occupancy type, height and occupant load, and educational buildings are treated as a high-priority occupancy because the occupants are children. The commonly cited figure for an educational building is an occupant load of 500 or more, but height, number of floors and basement use pull buildings in below that, and state rules add their own requirements on top. Tell us the building and we will tell you which way it falls before you commission anything.

How long does a campus audit take?

add

A single audit on a single-building school is usually most of a day. A full campus taking fire, canteen, child safety, transport, security and the SDG audit together is typically two to three days on site, depending on the number of blocks and the size of the fleet, plus the records review. The transport audit needs the buses on campus, so it is normally done early morning or after the afternoon run, and the SDG audit needs meter readings and bills, so it helps if somebody pulls the last twelve months out before we arrive.

Do you need to speak to our staff and see our records?

add

Yes, and an audit that does not is not worth buying. We ask for staff police verification records, drill logs, the AMC and service records for fire equipment, vehicle papers, the canteen contract and the contractor’s FSSAI licence, the child protection policy, and for the SDG audit twelve months of electricity and water bills and your waste vendor’s authorisation. We speak to the people who would actually be involved — the gate, the drivers, the mess staff, class teachers — rather than only to the management, because the difference between the two answers is most of what the audit finds.

Will you tell us what you find, or just write it down?

add

Both, and in that order. Anything urgent is said on the day, on site, to whoever can act on it. Anything that looks like it needs a lawyer, a licensed contractor or a report to an authority is flagged as such rather than left to be inferred from a paragraph in a report. The written report follows within a few working days.

What does a campus audit cost?

add

It is scoped before it is quoted. Number of blocks, number of students, how many kitchens and buses, and how many of the six audits you want all change the number, so a published figure would be a fiction. Tell us the campus on WhatsApp or by email and you will have a scope and a figure before anything is booked.
